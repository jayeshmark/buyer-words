# Buyer Words: Forest — the buyer's own phrases, sorted, with draft ad lines

Product: **Forest: Focus for Productivity** (iOS) / **Forest: Stay Focused** (Android), by SEEKRTECH CO., LTD. A focus / Pomodoro app: plant a virtual tree, it grows while you stay off your phone, it withers if you leave.
Run of the "Start With Their Words" skill, 2026-10-22. Every verbatim below was copied exactly from a real review read during the run; spelling, slang and typos are the reviewers' own. Raw text with full reviews is in the working file `skill-test-buyer-words-raw.md`.

Marking used in the piles: **[REPEAT]** = the same idea appears in two or more different mouths (a pattern, not one person). **🔥 BURN** = the phrase carries strong emotion (anger, shame, relief, pride).

## Part 3 — Phrase to line pairs

Each line is a rearrangement of the buyer's phrase beside it — cut, reordered, or a problem phrase joined to a desire phrase. The buyer's words stay inside the line.

1. **Phrase:** "it stays there as a monument to my lack of commitment" (App Store, of the withered tree)
   **Line:** "The tree you kill stays in your forest — a monument to your lack of commitment."

2. **Phrase:** "this is the only thing that motivates me to study!" (Google Play)
   **Line:** "The only thing that motivates me to study."

3. **Phrase:** "I'm an unmedicated ADHD adult. I've struggled with executive function for a long time. This has made a big difference." (App Store, via forestapp.cc)
   **Line:** "Unmedicated ADHD. A long time struggling with executive function. This made a big difference."

4. **Phrase:** "Today alone, I've studied for 5 hours and gotten so much done thanks to wanting to keep my tree alive" (App Store)
   **Line:** "Five hours studied today — because I wanted to keep my tree alive."

5. **Phrase:** "when I go on social media during breaks, it is hard to go back to studying" + "they give a meditation too during the break time" (Google Play)
   **Line:** "A break that ends in meditation, not social media — so you can actually go back to studying."

6. **Phrase:** "I would always use my phone rather than utilizing my off time" + "love having something to work towards as I study" (App Store)
   **Line:** "Stop using your phone instead of your off time. Grow something to work towards."

7. **Phrase:** "I just need a little reminder not to automatically pick up my phone and this app does the trick!" (App Store)
   **Line:** "Need a reminder not to automatically pick up your phone? This app does the trick."

8. **Phrase:** "the deep focus mode stops me from getting distracted anytime" (Google Play)
   **Line:** "Deep Focus stops you getting distracted. Anytime."

9. **Phrase:** "I made all my friends get this app" (Google Play)
   **Line:** "The focus app you'll make all your friends get."

10. **Phrase:** "this app helps me focus as I feel committed to grow my forest, and so I grew my productivity so far" (Google Play)
    **Line:** "Commit to your forest. Grow your productivity."

11. **Phrase:** "you can save up in-game coins to plant REAL TREES!!!" (App Store)
    **Line:** "Stay focused. Save coins. Plant REAL trees."

12. **Phrase (objection pile):** "spits in the face of people who paid the one time payment for Pro features" (Google Play)
    **Line (objection-handling copy, e.g. a pricing page — NOT a headline):** "Paid for Pro once? You should never be asked to pay for it again."
    ⚠ *Caution: this line promises something Forest currently does not do — users are being moved to a subscription. A line built from an objection can accidentally promise the opposite of the product's real behaviour; it belongs in a "fix the product / answer the objection" conversation, not in an ad, until it is true.*

## Part 4 — The three strongest lines

1. **"The tree you kill stays in your forest — a monument to your lack of commitment."**
   Strongest because it is a burn (shame and pride in one phrase) that names the product's core mechanic in a metaphor the buyer invented and no competitor can borrow.

2. **"The only thing that motivates me to study."**
   Strongest because it is a repeat across both stores ("only thing" / "sole thing") — the market saying the same superlative twice — and it needed no rearrangement at all.

3. **"Unmedicated ADHD. A long time struggling with executive function. This made a big difference."**
   Strongest because it is a burn (relief after a long struggle) that names a precise audience and its outcome entirely in that audience's own clinical words.

## Part 5 — What could not be reached, stated plainly

- **The App Store page itself** (apps.apple.com) was fetched, but its fetched text contained **no user reviews** — the reviews sit behind interaction a text fetch cannot perform. App Store verbatims above come from a mirror of the store's review feed (WorldsApps) and from store reviews quoted on the product's own site.
- **The Google Play listing page itself** was fetched and likewise returned listing copy only, no review text. Play verbatims come from Play listing crawls and AppBrain's mirror of Play comments.
- **AppBrain's iOS page and AppRecs** were fetched; both carried listing copy only, no reviews.
- **Reddit:** two searches, including a site-restricted one, surfaced no Forest discussion threads, so no Reddit verbatims are used. The skill lists Reddit as a mining source; in this run it produced nothing findable.
- **YouTube comments:** not reached — no readable comment text surfaced through search or fetch.
- **Volume:** roughly 63 reviews were read, against the skill's aim of at least 100 per major source. The depth test (stating frustrations and desires in the customers' own words) was met; the volume aim was not.
- **Languages:** both stores carry Forest reviews in many languages (Arabic and Spanish ones were read on the U.S. mirror page). The skill gives no instruction on non-English verbatims; only English phrases were mined, which skews the piles toward English-speaking users.
- **Vendor curation:** the forestapp.cc quotes are all five stars and were selected by the seller. They are marked as such wherever used.
- One long first-person magazine essay about Forest (The Awl, 2018) holds strong user language but is neither a store review nor a community post, so it was left out of the piles.
